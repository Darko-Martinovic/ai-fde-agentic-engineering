## Built

- Configuration: `settings/.env.defaults` (committed) holds every setting that isn't secret, such as hosts, ports, the database file and the model; `.env` holds the keys and overrides it; the environment overrides both. `settings/models.toml` lists the models allowed, as `provider:model` with their prices. `settings/__init__.py` and `settings/settings.config.ts` read them; a value can name another as `${NAME}`. Tests and the end-to-end run read `settings/.env.defaults` alone.
- `backend/`: UnderCover's records on Supabase's Postgres when `DATABASE_URL` is set (in `.env`), in the `undercover` schema, and in SQLite otherwise, as in the tests. The tables are SQLAlchemy Core `Table`s in `backend/db.py` and every query is a Core expression in `backend/store/`, written once for both. On Postgres each process keeps a pool of at most 4 connections (`DATABASE_CONNECTIONS`), waiting up to two minutes for one, since many claim agents at once share it, the search path is set to the schema on every connection, and the tables have row level security on, with no policies, so Supabase's API roles can't reach them. Each request's work is one transaction, committed before the response. The records are seeded from `data/` the first time a process finds no tables, with read operations for the claims queue (each claim's state, latest decision and payment), one claim, a policy with its plan and add-ons, a customer with their claim history, the policy wording, the claim agent's instructions (`backend/instructions/claim.md`, written as goals) and the handlers. The policy wording and the claims manual are split into their numbered sections: `list_sections` (each document's opening, its version and scope, is section 0), `search_documents` (the three sections that best match a query, by the words they share, a title's words counting twice) and `read_section`. `calculate_payout` works out an approval's payout on a claim's policy with UnderCover's payout rules (`backend/payout.py`, from `labs/week2day1_payout.py`: per-item and electronics limits, cash, hotel nights by the room, meals without receipts, what others paid, the deductible, the benefit maximum), step by step, and whether it needs a sign-off under the limit in `data/company.json`; it records nothing. The backend never reads `data/evals/` or `data/security/`.
- Runs (`backend/runs.py`, `backend/run_views.py`): a run is a dispatch, with its N claim agents at once (1 to `MAX_AGENTS`, 25), its model and reasoning effort, a status (running, finished or stopped), its start and finish, and its claims in order (`run_claims`). Only the agent's service writes them: `create_dispatch`, `record_run_claims`, `finish_run` (finished, or stopped when a handler asked it to stop) and `stop_runs` (every run still running, as the service does when it starts). `get_latest_dispatch` gives the newest with each claim's line from the queue and its start, seconds and model cost in the run, the run's model calls, tokens and cost, and its seconds once it ends. `finish_run` builds a dispatch's summary from its claims as they stand, each counted once: approved (a payout held for sign-off counts as approved), declined and referred, the payouts waiting for a sign-off, the claims left for a handler, and the unfinished ones, in progress or waiting, for the next dispatch. `list_models` (`backend/agent_models.py`) lists the models in `settings/models.toml`, marking `MODEL_NAME`.
- The claim workflow (`backend/workflow.py`): `start_claim` (in a running run, or on its own with no run), `record_step`, `list_steps`, `decide_claim`, `pay_claim` and `fail_claim` (`backend/workflow.py`, the agent's moves), and `review_payout` and `decide_as_handler` (`backend/reviews.py`, the handlers'), following the rules in `backend/claim_rules.py`. A handler reviews a held payout with `review_payout`: a sign-off records who and when on the decision and puts the claim back in progress for the agent to pay, and the payment records the handler and the time of the sign-off; a rejection needs a note, which goes back to the agent, which decides again. After a rejection the agent's next decision always goes back to a handler (an approval of any size waits for a sign-off, anything else is referred), a second rejection refers the claim, and a failure after a rejection leaves the claim for a handler. Once a review is committed, a background task asks the agent's service to pick the claim up (`backend/agent_service.py`, at `UNDERCOVER_AGENT`); if the service can't be reached, the backend logs a warning and the review stands. A repeat of the agent's decision or of a payment already recorded, as when the agent carries on after a restart, returns the claim, and a different one is refused; a claim's `revision` makes sure two at once are recorded once. A claim reports its latest attempt's run and thread and how many attempts it has had. A claim moves from `waiting` to `in_progress`, then to `awaiting_sign_off` (approved over $1,000), `referred` or `decided`. A claim that fails before it is decided goes back to `waiting`, with the failure recorded as a step; on its second failure it is `left_for_handler`. A claim with a decision is never failed (the next run carries it on), except one whose payout a handler rejected, which is left for a handler. A handler decides a referred claim or one left for them. A payment over $1,000 is refused until a handler signs off, and a referral waits for a handler to approve or decline it. Payments are recorded, not made.
- Cost and time (`backend/usage.py`): `record_model_call` records each of a claim agent's model calls on the claim, in `model_calls` (its thread, its run or none, the model, the input and output tokens, the cost in US dollars and the seconds), and `record_work_time` each spell of the agent's work on a claim, in `work_times` (its thread, its run or none, and its seconds, with its start worked out as the time it's recorded less its seconds). Both need a claim that has been started (409), a known claim and a known run or none (404), and no number below zero (422). A spell is one run of the claim graph, until it ends or pauses for a handler, so a wait for a sign-off never counts. The cost is OpenRouter's, as each reply reports it (`token_usage.cost`), or else the model's prices in `settings/models.toml`, as for Anthropic and Ollama. A claim can be started with no run (`start_claim` with `run_id` null), as a claim worked on its own will be, and a run's usage adds up its claims' model calls, failed attempts and retries included. After a handler's review the agent's service carries the claim on with no run, so a run's costs are its own. A Supabase database seeded before this needs `uv run -m backend.seed` again, for the new tables.
- `agent/`: the UnderCover Agent, in five parts (`docs/CODE_STANDARDS.md` 13). The dispatcher (`agent/dispatcher.py`) is a LangGraph graph with no model, in the style of `labs/week2day4.ipynb`: `list_claims` lists the queue's claims in progress, then the waiting ones, oldest submitted first, leaving out any the service is carrying on after a review, and records them with the dispatch; `fan_out` sends each to `work_claim` with a `Send`, or goes straight to `finish_run` when there are none; LangGraph runs up to N `work_claim` tasks at once (`max_concurrency`); and `finish_run` runs once after every claim, finishing the run or marking it stopped. `work_claim` calls `harness.work_in_run`, the one way a dispatch reaches the claim agent, which carries a claim in progress on or starts a waiting one, with a checkpointer of its own; a claim whose first attempt failed and went back in the queue is worked once more. Its `RetryPolicy` tries a claim three times in all on a rate limit or a timeout (`harness.TRANSIENT`), waiting about 10 seconds then 20, and each retry carries the claim on from its last saved step as the same attempt, since the harness lets those errors through without settling the claim. On the last try, and for any other error, it reports the claim "left for the next dispatch" with the error and leaves it as it stands, so no claim stops the dispatch; LangGraph 1.2.12's `error_handler` re-raises a handled error when two workers fail side by side, which a test records. Once a handler asks it to stop, `work_claim` starts no more claims and works none once more; the claims already working finish their spell. The dispatcher saves no progress of its own: each claim saves its own on its thread, and the next dispatch carries on what a stopped one left. The claim agent is a LangGraph graph of our own (`agent/graph.py`), as in `labs/week2day3.ipynb`: its state (`ClaimState`: the messages, the model calls made, and the decision and status the backend recorded), an `agent` node that calls the model with the tools bound, a `tools` node (LangGraph's `ToolNode`), a `wait_for_sign_off` node that pauses with `interrupt()`, and a `pay` node. The agent works the claim with its tools and records its decision with `decide_claim`, which ends its work; the graph then follows the backend's reply: it pays an approval the backend can pay at once, pauses when the backend says the approval waits for a sign-off, and ends on a decline or a referral. It has no `pay_claim` tool, and the graph never compares a payout with the limit. Its progress is saved after every step (`durability="sync"`) with LangGraph's synchronous Postgres checkpointer (`agent/checkpoints.py`): `pool()` opens the process's pool of at most 4 connections, waiting up to two minutes for one, and sets the checkpointer's tables up once, with row level security on; `saver(pool)` gives each claim run a checkpointer of its own, since one lets a single read or write through at a time. The tests save in memory. Its instructions are in `backend/instructions/claim.md`, written as goals. In modules: `api.py` (the backend's API; its client retries a refused connection five times, as when `npm run dev` starts the backend beside the service), `tools.py` (the tools and the context they read), `graph.py`, `checkpoints.py`, `recording.py` (the steps, model calls and spells of work the claim agents record), `reports.py` (the line each piece of work on a claim reports), `harness.py` (the model, the claim graph for each claim, `work_claim`, `carry_on` and `work_in_run`), `dispatcher.py`, `service.py` and `config.py` (settings and models). It runs on OpenRouter, Anthropic or a local Ollama model. It reads the claim, the policy and the customer through the API, searches the policy wording and the claims manual and reads the sections it needs (its prompt lists their titles, not their text), works out an approval's payout with its `calculate_payout` tool and records its decision. Every tool call, tool result and reply is recorded as a step by the graph's nodes, and every section read as a `section` step. `work_claim(claim_id, run_id, model, saver)` works a claim as a new attempt on a new thread and reports in a line ("CLM-1001: approved $420.00, paid"); a provider error is retried twice (the model's `with_retry`), a claim is stopped after 30 model calls (counted in its saved state), and a failure before the decision, or a claim left undecided, goes to `fail_claim`. A claim with a decision is never failed: an approval whose payment failed stays in progress ("not paid yet"). Each of its model calls, and each spell of its work, is recorded on the claim with the run (see Cost and time). A refusal from the backend, or a tool called the wrong way, comes back to the agent as text.
- Tracing (`agent/tracing.py`): the agent's work is traced in Langfuse, from the keys in `.env` (`LANGFUSE_PUBLIC_KEY`, `LANGFUSE_SECRET_KEY`; `LANGFUSE_BASE_URL` and `LANGFUSE_TRACING_ENVIRONMENT`, `development`, are in `settings/.env.defaults`). Each claim worked is a `work-claim` trace of its own, input the claim and output its report, with the claim agent (`claim-agent`) inside it, in the session named after the claim's thread (such as `CLM-1001-b325c560`), so everything done on one claim is found together. A claim worked in a run is tagged with the run's id, such as `run-7`, so a whole run's claims are found together. It is its own trace even inside a dispatch, because the harness runs it in a fresh context, which also keeps LangGraph from taking the claim graph for a subgraph of the dispatcher: its checkpoints stay under its own thread. Every model call is a generation with the model, tokens and cost. A process that is killed leaves its traces unfinished. Without the keys nothing is sent and Langfuse warns that it's off; the tests never send.
- The agent's service (`agent/service.py`, `uv run -m agent.service`, on `AGENT_HOST` and `AGENT_PORT`, 8002): `health`; `start_dispatch` (N from 1 to 25 and a model from `settings/models.toml`), which answers 202 at once, or 409 with "A dispatch or an evaluation is already running", then creates the dispatch through the backend, with the model's reasoning effort, and works it with the dispatcher on a thread of its own, which prints each claim's report as it comes; a dispatcher that fails marks its run stopped, and either way the next start is free; `stop_run`, which asks the running dispatch to start no more claims and finish as stopped (409 when none runs); and `resume_claim`, which the backend calls after a handler's review. A resume answers 202 at once and carries the claim on, on a thread of its own, with `harness.carry_on`, on `MODEL_NAME`, outside any run, printing the claim's report: after a sign-off the graph resumes past its pause and pays (no model call); after a rejection the handler's note reaches the agent as a message and it decides again. It refuses (409) a claim that isn't in progress or that it is picking up already. While it carries a claim on, a dispatch leaves the claim alone, even one it listed before. Its lifespan keeps the process's pool open and marks any run a stopped service left running as stopped. A carry-on is a `carry-on` trace in the claim's session, beside the `work-claim` trace from before the pause. The dashboard can't start a dispatch yet; that comes in phase 3.
- `uv run -m agent CLM-1001` works one claim outside any run, starting it if it's waiting or carrying it on if it's in progress, on `MODEL_NAME` or `--model provider:model`. It needs `DATABASE_URL` for its saved progress. Don't run it on a claim a dispatch is working.
- `dashboard/`: the claims desk, in the brand's colors with light and dark themes.
  - Queue: the latest dispatch as it happens (its number and status, Running in Marigold, Finished in Sea Green or Stopped in Coral; when it started, its claim agents at once, its model, its claims done, its time and its model cost; each of its claims with a link, the customer, where it stands, its decision and payout, its time and its model cost; and, once it ends, what it left: "3 approved, 1 declined and 1 referred.", the payouts waiting for a sign-off, the claims left for a handler and those left for the next dispatch), or "No dispatch yet.", then a tile per state that filters the table, money totals, search, a type filter and a sortable table.
  - Claim: the claim in full (story, items, documents), the policy and customer, the decisions and payment, the sections the agent read, each opening in place, a live timeline of the agent's steps while the claim is in progress, a review form for a held payout (Sign off, or Reject with a note for the agent), a form for a handler to decide a referred claim or one left for a handler, and failed attempts among the steps.
  - Insights: what the agent's work adds up to, refreshed every five seconds: straight through, time per claim, a timeline of the latest run (a bar per claim from its first step to its last, colored by decision), tool calls and refusals, claims waiting for a handler, the money, the agent's decisions by claim type, the tools it called, the policy sections it cited, and a table of every claim it worked.
  - Instructions: the claim agent's instructions, and the policy wording.
- Insights (`backend/insights.py`): `get_insights` sums up the agent's work on the queue from the records already kept: claims decided, straight through (declined, or approved and paid by the agent), sign-offs, referrals, claims waiting for a handler, tool calls and the calls the backend refused, time per claim (first to last step of its latest run), money, decisions by claim type, tools and the policy sections cited.
- `bench/`: the model comparison, apart from the product. For each trial in `bench/plan.toml` (a model, the 25 claims or the five `tiny` ones, a number of runs) it reseeds, works the claims one at a time through `harness.work_in_run` in a dispatch of their own, reads each claim's decision and payout and the dispatch's usage through the API, and scores them against the handlers' decisions in `data/evals/decisions.json`: the decision, and an approval's payout within a dollar. Only `bench/score.py` reads `data/evals/`, and a test checks it. Runs are kept in `bench/results.json`; a run the provider refused outright is tried again and not scored. It refuses to pass `BENCH_BUDGET_USD` ($10). It writes `bench/results.md` and the chart `bench/results.svg`.
- `e2e/`: Playwright tests of the queue, opening a claim, a sign-off and a rejection reaching the agent's service (a stand-in for it, `e2e/agent-stand-in.ts`, on port 8003), deciding a referral, the agent's steps on a claim page, Insights and the dispatch panel following a dispatch played out through the API, on their own backend (port 8001), dashboard (port 5174) and database (`e2e/undercover-e2e.db`, seeded fresh each run).
- `backend/postman/`: a Postman collection and environment for the whole API, including a dispatch through the workflow, its claims, its finish and summary, and the insights it adds up to.

## Running it

- `npm run dev` starts the backend (`uv run -m backend`), the agent's service (`uv run -m agent.service`) and the dashboard on the hosts and ports in `settings/.env.defaults`: by default the backend on port 8000, documented at `/docs`, the agent's service on port 8002 and the dashboard on port 5173. On Windows the backend's auto-reload sees a change but can fail to restart, leaving the old code running; after pulling or switching branches, restart `npm run dev`.
- The database is Supabase when `.env` has `DATABASE_URL` (the session pooler's connection string), and otherwise the SQLite file named in `UNDERCOVER_DB`, `backend/undercover.db` by default. `uv run -m backend.seed` resets the backend's tables in whichever it is to the data in `data/` ("Reset the undercover schema on Postgres"), leaving any other tables alone. The tests and the end-to-end run never read `.env`, so they always use SQLite. The agent needs Supabase: it saves its progress there, so without `DATABASE_URL` the agent's service doesn't start and `uv run -m agent` stops before it begins; the backend and the dashboard still work on SQLite.
- Work survives a restart. A dispatch carries on every claim left in progress before the waiting ones: a claim the agent was stopped on carries on from its last saved step on the same thread, without a new attempt, and a claim signed off while the agent's service was away is paid. A claim that fails while carried on goes back in the queue and the same dispatch starts it afresh on a new thread. A claim that can't be picked up at all, such as one whose decision was made through the API so the graph saved nothing, is reported ("not picked up") and stays in progress for a handler to look at; it never stops a dispatch. When the agent's service starts, it marks a dispatch left running as stopped.
- The default model is `openrouter:openai/gpt-6-luna`, with low reasoning effort, the brief's choice (every model runs with the options `settings/models.toml` gives it). `MODEL_NAME` in `.env` overrides it, such as `anthropic:claude-haiku-5-5` (needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`), `openrouter:openrouter/free` (free) or `ollama:qwen3.5:4b` (local and free).
- `uv run -m bench` runs the model comparison against the backend on port 8000, with no dispatch running: it resets the database before each run, so reseed or run it on data you don't need. `--estimate` prints what it would cost and stops; `--report` rewrites the report from the runs saved.
- `npm run check` runs every check, and passes without `.env`. The end-to-end tests need ports 8001, 5174 and 8003 free. Guards in `tests/test_standards.py` and `dashboard/src/standards.test.ts` hold the code to `docs/CODE_STANDARDS.md`.
- `npx -y newman@6 run backend/postman/undercover.postman_collection.json -e backend/postman/undercover_local.postman_environment.json` runs the Postman collection against the backend on port 8000. Its Workflow folder changes claims, so reset the data first, and its Agent folder calls the model for a moment.
