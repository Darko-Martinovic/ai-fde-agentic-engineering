## Built

- Configuration: `settings/.env.defaults` (committed) holds every setting that isn't secret, such as hosts, ports and the database file; `.env` holds the keys and overrides it; the environment overrides both. `data/models.json` lists the models the claim agent may use, on OpenRouter, with their reasoning efforts, prices and build options, and names version 1's model. `MODEL_NAME`, as `provider:model`, is only for a student on free models: set before seeding, it is the only model allowed, at reasoning effort `default` and a price of 0. `settings/__init__.py` and `settings/settings.config.ts` read them; a value can name another as `${NAME}`. Tests and the end-to-end run read `settings/.env.defaults` alone.
- `backend/`: UnderCover's records on Supabase's Postgres when `DATABASE_URL` is set (in `.env`), in the `undercover` schema, and in SQLite otherwise, as in the tests. The tables are SQLAlchemy Core `Table`s in `backend/db.py` and every query is a Core expression in `backend/store/`, written once for both. On Postgres each process keeps a pool of at most 4 connections (`DATABASE_CONNECTIONS`), waiting up to 25 seconds for one (`DATABASE_WAIT_SECONDS`, under the agent's 30 second wait for the backend), since many claim agents at once share it, the search path is set to the schema on every connection, and the tables have row level security on, with no policies, so Supabase's API roles can't reach them. Each request's work is one transaction, committed before the response. The records are seeded from `data/` the first time a process finds no tables, with read operations for the claims queue (each claim's state, latest decision and payment), one claim, a policy with its plan and add-ons, a customer with their claim history, the policy wording, the claim agent's instructions (`backend/instructions/claim.md`, written as goals) and the handlers. The policy wording and the claims manual are split into their numbered sections: `list_sections` (each document's opening, its version and scope, is section 0), `search_documents` (the three sections that best match a query, by the words they share, a title's words counting twice) and `read_section`. `calculate_payout` works out an approval's payout on a claim's policy with UnderCover's payout rules (`backend/payout.py`, from `labs/week2day1_payout.py`: per-item and electronics limits, cash, hotel nights by the room, meals without receipts, what others paid, the deductible, the benefit maximum), step by step, and whether it needs a sign-off under the limit in `data/company.json`; it records nothing. The backend never reads `data/security/`, and of `data/evals/` only seeding reads the answer key (see Evaluations).
- Sign-in (`backend/auth.py`, `backend/signed_in.py`, `backend/handlers.py`): handlers sign in to the dashboard through Supabase Auth, and the backend checks the access token on every handler action: `review_payout`, `decide_as_handler`, `start_dispatch`, `stop_run` and `start_evaluation`. `auth.py`, the only module that reaches Supabase Auth, checks a token against the project's public keys at `/auth/v1/.well-known/jwks.json` under `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` with PyJWT's `PyJWKClient` (ES256 or RS256, audience `authenticated`, an email and an expiry, not expired, with 30 seconds for the two clocks to differ), and returns its email. `signed_in.py`'s `SignedIn` checks the token before it takes a database connection and finds the handler by its email on a short connection of its own, so a route that goes on to ask the agent's service holds none (401 "Sign in as a handler first" without a valid token, 403 when no handler has the email), and `senior(action)` refuses a handler who can't sign off payouts over the limit with a 403 that names the action and the handler ("Only a handler who can sign off payouts over $1,000.00 can review this payout. Marcus Oyelaran can't."). Reviewing a held payout, either way, and approving a referral over the limit are senior actions; Dana Whitfield is the senior handler, and Marcus Oyelaran and Priya Raman aren't (`data/handlers.json`). `get_signed_in_handler` (`GET /api/me`) returns the handler signed in, and `list_handlers` names the handlers without their emails; reads stay open, as the agent's service reads without signing in. The handlers' table keeps each email, which seeding stores. The agent's service's own writes under `/api/runs` stay open until Week 3.
- The claim agent's configuration, in versions (`backend/configuration.py`, `backend/store/versions.py`, `backend/diffs.py`, `backend/db_improvement.py`): a version is the claim agent's instructions, model and reasoning effort, with the version it was based on, why, who proposed it ("The claims team" for version 1, or a round), who approved it and when, and its verdict and why. Seeding makes version 1, kept, from `backend/instructions/claim.md` on GPT-6 Luna at low (or the free model at `default`), and the models allowed (`models_allowed`) from `data/models.json` (`backend/free_model.py`, the only reader of `MODEL_NAME`). The current version is the newest kept one. `get_current_version` and `get_version` give a version as the claim agent runs it (instructions, model, reasoning effort, provider and build options, no scores); `get_configuration` lists every version, newest first, with its status (current, kept, under test or rolled back) and the lines it changed, and the models allowed; `get_version_details` adds its instructions and what it changed from the version it was based on, as stretches of added, removed and surrounding lines from `difflib.unified_diff` with two lines of context. Every dispatch and evaluation records the current version, with its model and reasoning effort.
- Settings (`backend/platform_settings.py`, `backend/settings_models.py`, `backend/store/platform_settings.py`): the platform's settings are kept as a history, one row per change with who made it and when: the approval limit, the business objectives on the held-out set (accuracy and grade floors), the noise and the tolerance for accuracy, the grade, cost per claim and time per claim, the spending limit for each round and the claim agents at once in a round's evaluations. Seeding makes the first row from `data/company.json` (the limit, the only reader of `payout_approval_limit`) and `data/improvement.json` (the rest, the lab's values and 25 agents). `get_settings` gives them with the models allowed; `change_settings` (a full set, validated: a limit and a spending limit above 0, floors of 0 to 1 and 0 to 3, margins of 0 or more, 1 to `MAX_AGENTS` agents) is a senior action. Every decision records the approval limit it was recorded under, and a payout over that limit waits for a sign-off, so a new limit applies to the decisions after it.
- Runs (`backend/runs.py`, `backend/run_views.py`): `start_dispatch` (N from 1 to 25, on the current version) and `stop_run` ask the agent's service through `backend/agent_service.py` and write nothing: they answer 202, pass on the service's refusal, such as its 409, and answer 503 with "The agent's service isn't running" when it can't be reached, or 502 with "The agent's service failed" when it fails or doesn't answer in time. A run is a dispatch, with its N claim agents at once (1 to `MAX_AGENTS`, 25), its version of the claim agent's configuration with its model and reasoning effort, a status (running, finished or stopped), its start and finish, and its claims in order (`run_claims`). Only the agent's service writes them: `create_dispatch`, `record_run_claims`, `finish_run` (finished, or stopped when a handler asked it to stop) and `stop_runs` (every run still running, as the service does when it starts). `get_latest_dispatch` gives the newest with each claim's line from the queue and its start, seconds and model cost in the run, the run's model calls, tokens and cost, and its seconds once it ends. `finish_run` builds a dispatch's summary from its claims as they stand, each counted once: approved (a payout held for sign-off counts as approved), declined and referred, the payouts waiting for a sign-off, the claims left for a handler, and the unfinished ones, in progress or waiting, for the next dispatch.
- The claim workflow (`backend/workflow.py`): `start_claim` (in a running run, or on its own with no run), `record_step`, `list_steps`, `decide_claim`, `pay_claim` and `fail_claim` (`backend/workflow.py`, the agent's moves), and `review_payout` and `decide_as_handler` (`backend/reviews.py`, the handlers'), following the rules in `backend/claim_rules.py`. A handler reviews a held payout with `review_payout`: a sign-off records who and when on the decision and puts the claim back in progress for the agent to pay, and the payment records the handler and the time of the sign-off; a rejection needs a note, which goes back to the agent, which decides again. After a rejection the agent's next decision always goes back to a handler (an approval of any size waits for a sign-off, anything else is referred), a second rejection refers the claim, and a failure after a rejection leaves the claim for a handler. Once a review is committed, a background task asks the agent's service to pick the claim up (`backend/agent_service.py`, at `UNDERCOVER_AGENT`); if the service can't be reached, the backend logs a warning and the review stands. A repeat of the agent's decision or of a payment already recorded, as when the agent carries on after a restart, returns the claim, and a different one is refused; a claim's `revision` makes sure two at once are recorded once. A claim reports its latest attempt's run and thread and how many attempts it has had. A claim moves from `waiting` to `in_progress`, then to `awaiting_sign_off` (approved over $1,000), `referred` or `decided`. A claim that fails before it is decided goes back to `waiting`, with the failure recorded as a step; on its second failure it is `left_for_handler`. A claim with a decision is never failed (the next run carries it on), except one whose payout a handler rejected, which is left for a handler. A handler decides a referred claim or one left for them. A payment over $1,000 is refused until a handler signs off, and a referral waits for a handler to approve or decline it. Payments are recorded, not made.
- Evaluations (`backend/evaluations.py`, `backend/scoring.py`, `backend/store/evaluations.py`): seeding stores the held-out claims (`held_out_claims`, out of the queue) and the handlers' decisions on both labelled sets (`handler_decisions`: the set, the claim, the decision, the payout and whether it's one of the five `tiny`), from `data/evals/`, which nothing else reads; no claim operation reads these tables. `start_evaluation` (a set, `evaluation` or `held_out`, all of it or its tiny claims, N and a model) asks the agent's service, as `start_dispatch` does. `create_evaluation`, which the service calls, records the run and a waiting copy of each of the set's claims, oldest submitted first, with an id such as `CLM-1104-E7` written into its record too, `evaluation_id` set to the run and `copy_of` to the original. Copies work through every claim operation, so the claim agent works them as any claim; the queue, Insights and a dispatch leave them out, and nobody can review or decide one (`review_payout` and `decide_as_handler` refuse it with a 409); the claim agent's `get_claim` tool leaves out which evaluation a copy is in, so a copy reads as any claim. `list_evaluations` (newest first) and `get_evaluation` score an evaluation when it's read, from the agent's own decisions on its copies, with the lab's measures: `decision_accuracy`, `accuracy` (the decision and a payout within a dollar), `cost_of_mistakes` ($1,500 for a wrongful decline, the payout for a wrongful approval whether or not it was paid, $50 for a needless referral, the difference on a right approval), `straight_through`, `model_cost_per_claim`, `seconds_per_claim` and `run_seconds`, a claim the agent didn't decide counting as a referral; and each copy beside the handler's decision and payout, with the agent's reasons, whether it's right and its mistake cost. The agent's API has no function for the scoreboard, and nothing the agent reads holds the handlers' words, which tests check.
- The judge (`backend/judge.py`, `backend/grading.py`, `backend/store/grades.py`): Jev (`typesafe/jev-1.13`), through OpenRouter's Decisions endpoint with the `openrouter` package, at `JUDGE_URL`, grades each evaluated copy's explanation against the handler's reason and reference, which seeding now stores from `data/evals/` (nothing the claim agent reads holds them): the state is the handler's decision, reason and reference beside the agent's decision, reason and section, and the lab's three questions in one call give the score from 0 (F) to 3 (A), its most likely level as the letter, and the probabilities of the same grounds and the same section. Each call has 10 seconds and no retries; any error leaves the copy ungraded, and it's logged. `finish_run` grades an evaluation that isn't stopped, one thread per copy, holding no database connection while the judge answers; an undecided copy is an F without a call, and with `MODEL_NAME` set nothing is graded. An evaluation's measures add its grade, the average over its graded copies or none, and the judge's cost, apart from the claim agents'.
- Cost and time (`backend/usage.py`): `record_model_call` records each of a claim agent's model calls on the claim, in `model_calls` (its thread, its run or none, the model, the input and output tokens, the cost in US dollars and the seconds), and `record_work_time` each spell of the agent's work on a claim, in `work_times` (its thread, its run or none, and its seconds, with its start worked out as the time it's recorded less its seconds). Both need a claim that has been started (409), a known claim and a known run or none (404), and no number below zero (422). A spell is one run of the claim graph, until it ends or pauses for a handler, so a wait for a sign-off never counts. The cost is OpenRouter's, as each reply reports it (`token_usage.cost`), or nothing for a local model. A claim can be started with no run (`start_claim` with `run_id` null), as a claim worked on its own will be, and a run's usage adds up its claims' model calls, failed attempts and retries included. After a handler's review the agent's service carries the claim on with no run, so a run's costs are its own. A Supabase database seeded before this needs `uv run -m backend.seed` again, for the new tables.
- `agent/`: the UnderCover Agent, in five parts (`docs/CODE_STANDARDS.md` 13). The dispatcher (`agent/dispatcher.py`) is a LangGraph graph with no model, in the style of `labs/week2day4.ipynb`, and works dispatches and evaluations alike: `list_claims` takes an evaluation's copies, which the backend made, or for a dispatch lists the queue's claims in progress, then the waiting ones, oldest submitted first, leaving out any the service is carrying on after a review, and records them with the dispatch; `fan_out` sends each to `work_claim` with a `Send`, or goes straight to `finish_run` when there are none; LangGraph runs up to N `work_claim` tasks at once (`max_concurrency`); and `finish_run` runs once after every claim, finishing the run or marking it stopped. `work_claim` calls `harness.work_in_run`, the one way a dispatch reaches the claim agent, which carries a claim in progress on or starts a waiting one, with a checkpointer of its own, and clears the saved progress of an evaluation's copy held for sign-off, since nobody signs a copy off and its approval counts as finished; a claim whose first attempt failed and went back in the queue is worked once more. Its `RetryPolicy` tries a claim three times in all on a rate limit or a timeout (`harness.TRANSIENT`), waiting about 10 seconds then 20, and each retry carries the claim on from its last saved step as the same attempt, since the harness lets those errors through without settling the claim. On the last try, and for any other error, it reports the claim "left for the next dispatch" with the error and leaves it as it stands, so no claim stops the dispatch; LangGraph 1.2.12's `error_handler` re-raises a handled error when two workers fail side by side, which a test records. Once a handler asks it to stop, `work_claim` starts no more claims and works none once more; the claims already working finish their spell. The dispatcher saves no progress of its own: each claim saves its own on its thread, and the next dispatch carries on what a stopped one left. The claim agent is a LangGraph graph of our own (`agent/graph.py`), as in `labs/week2day3.ipynb`: its state (`ClaimState`: the messages, the model calls made, and the decision and status the backend recorded), an `agent` node that calls the model with the tools bound, a `tools` node (LangGraph's `ToolNode`), a `wait_for_sign_off` node that pauses with `interrupt()`, and a `pay` node. The agent works the claim with its tools and records its decision with `decide_claim`, which ends its work; the graph then follows the backend's reply: it pays an approval the backend can pay at once, pauses when the backend says the approval waits for a sign-off, and ends on a decline or a referral. It has no `pay_claim` tool, and the graph never compares a payout with the limit. Its progress is saved after every step (`durability="sync"`) with LangGraph's synchronous Postgres checkpointer (`agent/checkpoints.py`): `pool()` opens the process's pool of at most 4 connections, waiting up to 25 seconds for one, and sets the checkpointer's tables up once, with row level security on; `saver(pool)` gives each claim run a checkpointer of its own, since one lets a single read or write through at a time. The tests save in memory. Its instructions are in `backend/instructions/claim.md`, written as goals. In modules: `api.py` (the backend's API; its client retries a refused connection five times, as when `npm run dev` starts the backend beside the service), `tools.py` (the tools and the context they read), `graph.py`, `checkpoints.py`, `recording.py` (the steps, model calls and spells of work the claim agents record), `reports.py` (the line each piece of work on a claim reports), `harness.py` (the model, the claim graph for each claim, `work_claim`, `carry_on` and `work_in_run`), `dispatcher.py`, `service.py`, `versions.py` (a version as the harness builds its claim agents from it) and `connection.py` (the one connection to the backend). Each claim agent is built from a version of its configuration: its instructions and its model, on OpenRouter, or a free model in `MODEL_NAME` on OpenRouter or a local Ollama. It reads the claim, the policy and the customer through the API, searches the policy wording and the claims manual and reads the sections it needs (its prompt lists their titles, not their text), works out an approval's payout with its `calculate_payout` tool and records its decision. Every tool call, tool result and reply is recorded as a step by the graph's nodes, and every section read as a `section` step. `work_claim(claim_id, run_id, version, saver)` works a claim as a new attempt on a new thread and reports in a line ("CLM-1001: approved $420.00, paid"); a provider error is retried twice (the model's `with_retry`), a claim is stopped after 30 model calls (counted in its saved state), and a failure before the decision, or a claim left undecided, goes to `fail_claim`. A claim with a decision is never failed: an approval whose payment failed stays in progress ("not paid yet"). Each of its model calls, and each spell of its work, is recorded on the claim with the run (see Cost and time). A refusal from the backend, or a tool called the wrong way, comes back to the agent as text.
- Tracing (`agent/tracing.py`): the agent's work is traced in Langfuse, from the keys in `.env` (`LANGFUSE_PUBLIC_KEY`, `LANGFUSE_SECRET_KEY`; `LANGFUSE_BASE_URL` and `LANGFUSE_TRACING_ENVIRONMENT`, `development`, are in `settings/.env.defaults`). Each claim worked is a `work-claim` trace of its own, input the claim and output its report, with the claim agent (`claim-agent`) inside it, in the session named after the claim's thread (such as `CLM-1001-b325c560`), so everything done on one claim is found together. A claim worked in a run is tagged with the run's id, such as `run-7`, so a whole run's claims are found together. It is its own trace even inside a dispatch, because the harness runs it in a fresh context, which also keeps LangGraph from taking the claim graph for a subgraph of the dispatcher: its checkpoints stay under its own thread. Every model call is a generation with the model, tokens and cost. A process that is killed leaves its traces unfinished. Without the keys nothing is sent and Langfuse warns that it's off; the tests never send.
- The agent's service (`agent/service.py`, `uv run -m agent.service`, on `AGENT_HOST` and `AGENT_PORT`, 8002): `health`; `start_dispatch` (N from 1 to 25) and `start_evaluation` (a set, all of it or its tiny claims, and N), which answer 202 at once, or 409 with "A dispatch or an evaluation is already running", then create the run through the backend on the current version (an evaluation with its copies), and work it with claim agents built from the version the run records with the dispatcher on a thread of its own, which prints each claim's report as it comes; a dispatcher that fails marks its run stopped, and either way the next start is free; `stop_run`, which asks the running dispatch to start no more claims and finish as stopped (409 when none runs); and `resume_claim`, which the backend calls after a handler's review. A resume answers 202 at once and carries the claim on, on a thread of its own, with `harness.carry_on`, as the current version, outside any run, printing the claim's report: after a sign-off the graph resumes past its pause and pays (no model call); after a rejection the handler's note reaches the agent as a message and it decides again. It refuses (409) a claim that isn't in progress or that it is picking up already. While it carries a claim on, a dispatch leaves the claim alone, even one it listed before. Its lifespan keeps the process's pool open and marks any run a stopped service left running as stopped. A carry-on is a `carry-on` trace in the claim's session, beside the `work-claim` trace from before the pause.
- `uv run -m agent CLM-1001` works one claim outside any run, as the current version, starting it if it's waiting or carrying it on if it's in progress. It needs `DATABASE_URL` for its saved progress. Don't run it on a claim a dispatch is working.

The header shows the handler's name and role and Sign out. A held payout's Sign off and Reject are the signed-in handler's, with no handler to choose, and a handler who can't sign off over the limit sees why in place of the buttons.
  - Queue: a form to start a dispatch ("Claim agents at once", 1 to 25 with 3 chosen, and Start a dispatch, on the current version), with Stop while a dispatch runs (then "Stopping: no more claims start, and those working finish." until it ends) and the backend's refusal in an alert; the latest dispatch as it happens (its number and status, Running in Marigold, Finished in Sea Green or Stopped in Coral; when it started, its claim agents at once, its model, its claims done, its time and its model cost; each of its claims with a link, the customer, where it stands, its decision and payout, its time and its model cost; and, once it ends, what it left: "3 approved, 1 declined and 1 referred.", the payouts waiting for a sign-off, the claims left for a handler and those left for the next dispatch), or "No dispatch yet.", then a tile per state that filters the table, money totals, search, a type filter and a sortable table.

## Running it

- `npm run dev` starts the backend (`uv run -m backend`), the agent's service (`uv run -m agent.service`) and the dashboard on the hosts and ports in `settings/.env.defaults`: by default the backend on port 8000, documented at `/docs`, the agent's service on port 8002 and the dashboard on port 5173. On Windows the backend's auto-reload sees a change but can fail to restart, leaving the old code running; after pulling or switching branches, restart `npm run dev`.
- The database is Supabase when `.env` has `DATABASE_URL` (the session pooler's connection string), and otherwise the SQLite file named in `UNDERCOVER_DB`, `backend/undercover.db` by default. `uv run -m backend.seed` resets the backend's tables in whichever it is to the data in `data/` ("Reset the undercover schema on Postgres"), leaving any other tables alone. The tests and the end-to-end run never read `.env`, so they always use SQLite. The agent needs Supabase: it saves its progress there, so without `DATABASE_URL` the agent's service doesn't start and `uv run -m agent` stops before it begins; the backend and the dashboard still work on SQLite.
- Work survives a restart. A dispatch carries on every claim left in progress before the waiting ones: a claim the agent was stopped on carries on from its last saved step on the same thread, without a new attempt, and a claim signed off while the agent's service was away is paid. A claim that fails while carried on goes back in the queue and the same dispatch starts it afresh on a new thread. A claim that can't be picked up at all, such as one whose decision was made through the API so the graph saved nothing, is reported ("not picked up") and stays in progress for a handler to look at; it never stops a dispatch. When the agent's service starts, it marks a dispatch left running as stopped.
- The claim agent runs as the current version of its configuration, GPT-6 Luna at low reasoning effort to begin with. A student on free models sets `MODEL_NAME` in `.env`, such as `openrouter:openrouter/free` or `ollama:qwen3.5:4b` (local), and reseeds: it is then the only model allowed and version 1's model.
- Signing in needs `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` in `.env`, and the handlers as users in the project's Supabase Auth (`labs/week2day5.ipynb`, Setting up for the product). `HANDLER_PASSWORD` in `.env` is their password, for browser checks and the Postman collection.
- `npm run check` runs every check, and passes without `.env`. The end-to-end tests need ports 8001, 5174 and 8003 free. Guards in `tests/test_standards.py` and `dashboard/src/standards.test.ts` hold the code to `docs/CODE_STANDARDS.md`.
- `npx -y newman@6 run backend/postman/undercover.postman_collection.json -e backend/postman/undercover_local.postman_environment.json --env-var supabaseUrl=… --env-var supabasePublishableKey=… --env-var handlerPassword=…` runs the Postman collection against the backend on port 8000, signing in as Dana Whitfield and Marcus Oyelaran through Supabase Auth before its requests. Its Workflow folder changes claims, so reset the data first.

## Known gaps

- At 25 claim agents at once, each claim takes about 2.5 times as long as at 3, though the model is no slower (2.07 against 1.98 seconds a call): the steps outside the model, the tool calls and the recording of each step through the backend and the checkpoint after each, most likely wait on the 4 connections each process keeps to Supabase. That is inferred from the model calls' times against the claims', not measured; the pools' own wait statistics (psycopg_pool's `get_stats()`, SQLAlchemy's checkouts) would show which pool it is. The dispatch as a whole still takes about as long as its slowest claim.
- A claim agent's reply can run long: in the live evaluation one GPT-6 Luna reply ran to 65,536 tokens and took over seven minutes, which set the run's time. `MODEL_TIMEOUT` didn't stop it, as it is a wait between bytes and the reply kept coming. GPT-6 Luna's replies are now capped at 16,000 tokens, in `data/models.json`.
- Langfuse's `GET /api/public/traces`, which the live check first reached for, answers 410: it is deprecated and stops working on November 16, 2026. A run's traces are found through `GET /api/public/v2/observations`, filtering on the `tags` column (`arrayOptions`, `any of`) with `fields=core,basic,trace_context`. The agent sends its traces through the SDK, which isn't affected.
- `uv run -m agent CLM-…` beside a dispatch can work the same claim at once; the backend still decides and pays it once. Stop doesn't interrupt a claim agent in the middle of its work. Old checkpoints and evaluations' copies are cleared only by a reseed. Scores aren't sent to Langfuse.
